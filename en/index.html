01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

How can a world model become a production tool?

World models can suggest a convincing world. Production needs measurable spatial evidence, persistent scene structure, editable assets, deterministic execution and validation.

My work explores the layer between those two: turning uncertain understanding into decisions a 3D pipeline can inspect and execute.

CASE 02 / GENERATED-ASSET REPLACEMENT

From a reference image
to an editable object.

ISOLATED CHAIR EXPERIMENT

A documented replacement workflow with a manual acceptance decision. This historical experiment is separate from the v2 floor case above.

The reconstruction provides a place to work. A generated mesh provides an object to edit. The challenge is deciding whether the two agree well enough to use together.

01 Prepare the reference02 Inspect the mesh03 Fit & diagnose04 Review & accept05 Return to the scene

01 / REFERENCE PREPARATION

Isolate what needs to be rebuilt.

The chair is first identified in the room image, then represented as a standalone appearance reference. This gives asset generation a focused subject instead of asking it to reproduce the entire room at once.

The prepared image helps describe the upholstery, silhouette and proportions. It is a generation input—not a measurement of the chair’s hidden geometry or its world-space position.

02 / CANDIDATE ASSET

Generate an object, then inspect it as geometry.

An external generative-3D tool supplies the mesh candidate. Multi-view inspection makes its silhouette, backrest, seat and base available for comparison before placement.

The exported axes and semantic directions need separate checks: a native “+Z view” is not automatically the front of the chair. The original inspection labels retain that uncertainty.

03 / SOURCE-SPACE FITTING

Appearance is a starting point. Placement needs evidence.

The candidate is fitted against the source reconstruction, checking position, orientation and uniform scale. The fragmented source chair supplies spatial evidence; the generated mesh supplies a more complete surface.

Projection and depth checks reveal disagreements that a clean asset preview can hide. A visually similar outline can still sit at the wrong depth or extend beyond the source surface.

WHAT THE CHECKS ACTUALLY SHOW

Two measurements.
Two different questions.

90.7% held-out surface samples
Within 0.05 source units after refitting. Position, orientation and uniform-scale checks passed in this chair experiment.
47.5% source-pixel depth support
The earlier diagnostic reports source pixels depth-supported at 0.05. This is a different test, not an alternative accuracy score or replacement success rate.

Source units are not calibrated meters. The review still found view-dependent depth differences and a backrest extending beyond the source geometry.

04 / REVIEW & ACCEPTANCE

A useful fit still needs an explicit decision.

The initial review kept the chair as a candidate because local mismatches remained. I then reviewed the in-scene comparison and accepted the replacement for this example using a more permissive production tolerance.

That decision changes the asset’s acceptance state; it does not erase the recorded differences or establish a universal threshold. Keeping the earlier rejection visible explains why human judgment is part of the workflow.

05 / SCENE REVIEW

Replace the object.
Keep the spatial context.

The supplied Blender views show the generated chair placed back into the room and available as a separate, selectable object.

Recorded comparison images—not a live browser reconstruction or compiler.

The soft, fragmented surroundings are retained reconstruction / Gaussian-scene reference layers, used to judge position, scale and layout. They are not the final asset geometry. Keeping references separate allows them to be hidden as individual meshes are generated, checked and placed.

MY CONTRIBUTION

Connecting generation to a scene decision.

I developed and tested the workflow around reference preparation, candidate inspection, source-space fitting, diagnostic comparisons and Blender scene review. External models provide the reconstruction and generated asset; my work connects those outputs and makes acceptance explicit.

CASE 03 / WALL GENERATION

Give the scene
an editable architectural shell.

SOURCE-CONSTRAINED EXPERIMENT

A room-scale geometry study. The reference image, source reconstruction and generated shell remain distinct representations.

Replacing furniture is only part of building a usable scene. The room also needs surfaces that can be inspected and edited independently of the noisy reconstruction.

01 / READ THE ROOM BOUNDARY

Compare the footprint at different heights.

The low, middle and high floor-relative slices show how the proposed wall segments relate to source-space samples. Looking across heights helps distinguish a recurring room boundary from local clutter. The dashed opening remains visible rather than being presented as a measured wall.

02 / CHECK THE PROJECTION

Review the shell against its source camera.

The comparison brings together the reference photo, Marble reconstruction, an unfurnished architectural shell and projected wall edges. The shell and edge overlay use the source camera, making their relationship to the reconstruction directly inspectable.

The reference-photo view is explicitly not independently calibrated. This is a source-space consistency check, not proof of metric accuracy against the photographed room.

03 / INSPECT THE GENERATED GEOMETRY

Separate architecture from appearance.

The interior and exterior views expose the room shell without furniture or reconstructed texture. This makes the wall shape, corners and opening easier to inspect before detailed assets are considered together in the scene.

Result: an inspectable architectural shell alongside its source evidence. These views document geometry generation and review; watertightness, collision behavior and physical dimensions require separate validation.

CASE 04 / MULTI-OBJECT REPLACEMENT

Bring the replacements
back into one room.

SCENE ASSEMBLY STUDY

Scene-level evidence, with reconstruction references still visible.

A single object can look convincing on its own. Multiple replacements need to work together: furniture must relate to the walls, equipment to the work surfaces, and every object to the surrounding layout.

FROM OBJECT FIT TO SCENE COHERENCE

Review relationships, not just individual silhouettes.

This assembly study extends the presentation beyond the chair to the room’s work surfaces, wall-mounted equipment and entrance structure. The Blender views show these elements together, so their relative scale, placement and visual alignment can be reviewed in context.

The remaining fragmented areas make the mixed state visible: generated geometry and retained reconstruction references coexist while the scene is being rebuilt.

WHAT THIS ADDS

Object, architecture and context.

The chair case shows an individual fitting and acceptance decision. The wall study adds an architectural shell. These assembly views bring the work into a shared scene for further inspection and editing.

WHAT STILL NEEDS VALIDATION

Scene-wide acceptance is a separate check.

The chair’s 90.7% surface-sample measurement applies only to that chair test. These multi-object views do not establish the same fit for every asset, automated batch acceptance, collision clearance or a completed v2 end-to-end pipeline.

05 / FAILURE → DIAGNOSIS → SYSTEM DESIGN

Where world models break.

The useful result is not only a better image.
It is knowing what the next system must control.

01 / SPATIAL UNCERTAINTY

Single-view depth ambiguity.

A plausible view leaves scale, occlusion and camera pose unresolved. Additional views do not help if they cannot be reliably registered.

Decision / Keep unsupported space explicit.

02 / REPRESENTATION GAP

Reconstruction is not production geometry.

The early mesh-reconstruction experiment in WORLDIR (~529k vertices / ~939k faces) was fragmented. A repaired surface still carried cost and topology limitations.

Decision / Inspect geometry, not just the preview.

03 / PLACEMENT AUTHORITY

An asset is not a scene decision.

A generated chair can look right and still disagree with source-space depth and silhouette. Appearance does not establish placement.

Decision / Fit and validate before acceptance.

07 / CAPABILITY BOUNDARIES

Different systems.
Different authority.

A pipeline becomes reliable when each component is responsible for what it can actually establish.

SYSTEMCAN PROVIDECANNOT DECIDE

01World Model

CAN PROVIDE

Semantics, object relationships and rough spatial hypotheses.

CANNOT DECIDE

Exact XYZ, metric scale or production geometry.

02Marble

CAN PROVIDE

Reconstruction evidence, camera cues and source-space observations.

CANNOT DECIDE

A trustworthy, production-ready mesh.

03Asset Generator

CAN PROVIDE

Candidate object geometry and appearance.

CANNOT DECIDE

Correct placement in the world.

04Scene Graph

CAN PROVIDE

Accepted decisions, provenance, confidence and state.

CANNOT DECIDE

Missing evidence. Recording a decision does not prove it true.

05Compiler

CAN PROVIDE

Deterministic execution of accepted scene decisions.

CANNOT DECIDE

Unknown spatial facts or absent constraints.

Responsibility model—not a claim that every stage of WORLDIR v2 is complete.
